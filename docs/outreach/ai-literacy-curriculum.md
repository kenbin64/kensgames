# It's Your AI
### A plain-language workshop series for everyday people

A free community curriculum. No tech background needed. The goal is not to sell
anyone on AI or to defend it. It is to help regular people understand what these
tools actually are, see that they do not have to settle for the version a company
hands them, and learn to shape one that reflects their own values, on their own
terms, with their judgment kept firmly in the driver's seat.

Written for a library, senior center, church hall, or community room. One
facilitator, a projector, and a room of curious people.

---

## Who this is for

Adults of any age who have heard about AI and feel some mix of curious, skeptical,
left behind, or uneasy. People who have never typed a prompt, and people who have
poked at a chatbot but feel it is "not for them." Assume zero jargon. Assume some
fear in the room, and treat it as reasonable, not silly.

## The one idea behind everything

> The version of AI you have been handed is **one setting someone else chose.**
> It is not the only one, and it is not the truth. You can understand it, shape it
> to your values, and stay in control of it. That is what this series teaches.

## The honesty rule (read before teaching)

This series earns trust by being straight. Three things we never do:

1. **We never oversell.** "Make it yours" means *steering and configuring* a tool,
   not rebuilding it, and not magic. Say so plainly.
2. **We never hide the failure modes.** It can be confidently wrong. It can be
   biased. It is not private by default. We show this on purpose.
3. **We keep the human in charge.** Every session ends on judgment: you decide,
   you verify, you own the result.

A facilitator who admits what AI is not will be believed about what it is.

## How to run it

- **Full series:** four sessions, 60 to 75 minutes each, one per week.
- **One-off talk:** a 50-minute standalone version is sketched at the end, drawn
  from Sessions 1 and 2, for when a venue wants to start with a single evening.
- **What to bring (facilitator):** a laptop, a projector, a free chatbot account
  for live demos, and the one-page handout printed for each attendee.
- **What attendees bring:** nothing required. A phone or laptop if they want to
  try along, but watching the screen is enough. No account needed to attend.
- **Tone:** plain, warm, unhurried. Lots of "let's try it and see." Questions
  welcome at any time, not just at the end.

## Session 1 — What AI Actually Is (and Isn't)

**Goal:** Replace fear and mystery with a plain, accurate mental model, and a
healthy dose of skepticism.

**By the end, attendees can:**
- Say in their own words what a chatbot is doing when it answers.
- Name three things it is *not* (a search engine, a fact machine, a person).
- Spot when an answer should not be trusted without checking.

**Agenda (about 60 min):**
1. *Welcome and the fear is fair (5 min).* Acknowledge the unease in the room.
   Nobody here is behind.
2. *What it actually is, in plain words (15 min).* A chatbot is a very good
   guesser of "what word probably comes next," trained by reading an enormous
   amount of human writing. That is the whole trick, and it is enough to be
   useful and to fool you. Analogy: the world's most well-read parrot, not a
   wizard and not a librarian.
3. *Live demo: make it be wrong (10 min).* Ask it something it will get
   confidently wrong (a made-up fact, a tricky number, a local detail). Let the
   room watch it state nonsense with total confidence. This single moment does
   more for safety than any warning.
4. *What it is NOT (10 min).* Not a search engine (it is not looking things up).
   Not a database of facts (it is guessing). Not a person (no feelings, no memory
   of you unless told). Not neutral (it reflects its training and whoever set it
   up).
5. *First gentle try (10 min).* Everyone who wants to types one friendly question.
   The point is just to remove the fear of the blank box.
6. *Close + take-home (10 min).* "This week, ask it one thing you already know
   the answer to, and judge how it did."

**Honest beat:** It sounds sure of itself even when it is wrong. Confidence is not
correctness. That is the most important thing you will learn all series.

## Session 2 — Making It Yours

**Goal:** Show that the default assistant is one configuration, and that anyone
can steer it toward their own voice, needs, and values.

**By the end, attendees can:**
- Write a simple instruction that changes how the AI talks to them.
- Give it their own information so it answers from their context, not the world's.
- Understand the difference between *steering* a tool and *trusting* it.

**Agenda (about 70 min):**
1. *Whose AI is it? (10 min).* The default chatbot was tuned by a company: its
   tone, its caution, its values. Useful, but it is *their* setting. You are
   allowed to change it.
2. *Demo: same question, different selves (15 min).* Ask one question with default
   settings. Then add a custom instruction: "Answer like a plain-spoken friend who
   respects my time and my faith, no hedging." Ask again. Let the room see the same
   tool become a different companion. This is the heart of the series.
3. *Your voice, your values (15 min).* Walk through writing a short "how I want you
   to treat me" note: tone, what matters to you, what to avoid. Attendees draft one
   line of their own.
4. *Give it your stuff (15 min).* Paste in a recipe, a letter, your notes, your
   situation, and watch it answer from *your* context instead of generic advice.
   The tool stops being a stranger and starts being yours.
5. *Close + take-home (10 min).* "This week, write three sentences telling it how to
   talk to you, and use them."

**Honest beat:** Shaping its *voice and focus* is easy and real. It still does not
*know* things and can still be wrong. You are changing the messenger, not turning
it into an oracle.

---

## Session 3 — Keeping Control and Staying Safe

**Goal:** Protect attendees' judgment, privacy, and dignity. This is the session
that makes the whole thing trustworthy.

**By the end, attendees can:**
- Decide what is and is not safe to type into a chatbot.
- Verify an answer before relying on it.
- Recognize AI used against them (scams, fake voices and images).

**Agenda (about 70 min):**
1. *Where your words go (15 min).* By default, what you type may be stored and used
   to improve the product. Plain rule: do not paste secrets, passwords, full
   account numbers, or other people's private details. Treat it like a helpful
   stranger, not a vault.
2. *The verify habit (15 min).* For anything that matters (health, money, legal,
   important facts), the answer is a starting point, never the final word. Demo:
   take an answer and check it against a trusted source together.
3. *Where NOT to lean on it (10 min).* Not your doctor, lawyer, or accountant.
   Great for drafting questions to ask the real ones; not a replacement for them.
4. *AI pointed at you (15 min).* Scam calls in a loved one's voice, fake images,
   too-perfect messages. What is now possible, and simple defenses: a family
   safe-word, slow down, call back on a known number, doubt urgency.
5. *Close + take-home (10 min).* "This week, set a family safe-word, and check one
   AI answer against a real source."

**Honest beat:** The same power that makes it yours can be turned against you by
others. Staying a little skeptical is not being behind. It is being wise.

---

## Session 4 — Your AI at Home

**Goal:** Turn understanding into everyday usefulness, and show the most private,
independent options, then send people off confident.

**By the end, attendees can:**
- Use AI for several real-life tasks well.
- Understand that a private version can run on their own computer, for free.
- Walk out with a small setup that is theirs.

**Agenda (about 70 min):**
1. *Everyday wins (20 min).* Live, practical demos the room calls out: write a
   hard letter, understand a confusing bill or form, plan a trip or a meal, learn
   something at your own pace, draft questions for a doctor or contractor.
2. *The privacy ladder (15 min).* From cloud chatbot, to custom instructions, to
   your own documents, to running a model entirely on your own computer where
   nothing leaves the house. Name a free option or two at a high level, so people
   know full independence is possible without a subscription.
3. *Build your own little setup (20 min).* Each attendee assembles their personal
   instruction note plus two or three go-to uses, written on the handout. They
   leave with a thing, not just ideas.
4. *Graduation and where to go next (15 min).* Recap the one idea: it is your AI,
   you shape it, you stay in charge. Invite them to the next round, and to bring a
   friend who is nervous about it.

**Honest beat:** The most powerful setup is not the fanciest. It is the one you
understand and control. Small and yours beats big and borrowed.

## The one-page take-home handout (content)

Print this for every attendee. Plain, fridge-worthy.

- **What it is:** a very good word-guesser trained on lots of writing. Useful, not
  all-knowing.
- **It can be confidently wrong.** Confidence is not correctness. Check anything
  that matters.
- **Make it yours:** tell it how to talk to you and what you value. Give it your
  own information.
- **Keep it safe:** no passwords, no full account numbers, no one else's private
  details.
- **Verify the important stuff** against a trusted source. It is a starting point,
  not the last word.
- **Watch for AI used against you:** fake voices and images. Use a family
  safe-word. Slow down. Call back on a known number.
- **You are in charge.** You decide, you check, you own the result.

## Standalone 50-minute version (single evening)

When a venue wants one talk before committing to a series:

1. *The fear is fair + what it actually is (12 min).* From Session 1.
2. *Make it be wrong, on purpose (8 min).* The confident-nonsense demo.
3. *It's their setting, not the only one (10 min).* The same-question-different-self
   demo from Session 2.
4. *Staying safe and in charge (12 min).* Privacy, verify, scams, in brief.
5. *You're not behind, and here's how to start this week (8 min).* Hand out the
   one-pager. Invite them to the full series.

---

## Facilitator prep checklist

- [ ] A free chatbot account, logged in and tested on the room's wifi beforehand.
- [ ] Pick the Session 1 "confidently wrong" question in advance and confirm it
      still fails (models change).
- [ ] Draft the Session 2 custom-instruction example in your own real voice.
- [ ] Print the one-page handout, enough for the room plus extras.
- [ ] Know one or two free local-AI options by name for Session 4, at a high level.
- [ ] Have a low-tech fallback (screenshots) in case the wifi dies.
- [ ] Open and close on the same sentence: *it is your AI, and you are in charge.*
