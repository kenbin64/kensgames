# Library pitch email

Copy-paste ready. Fill the [bracketed] bits. Keep it short; program coordinators
are busy and a tight, generous offer gets a faster yes.

## Who to send it to
The library's **adult programming**, **events**, or **community engagement**
coordinator. If you cannot find a name on the website, send to the general info
address and ask them to forward it to whoever handles adult programs. Attach
**Its_Your_AI_Handout.pdf** so they can see exactly what attendees would get.

## Subject line (pick one)
- Free community workshop: making sense of AI, in plain language
- Offer: a free, plain-language AI workshop for your patrons
- Free digital-literacy program: "It's Your AI"

## Email body

Hello [Name],

I am a local software engineer in [city], and I would like to offer a free,
plain-language workshop to help your patrons understand AI: what it actually is,
what it is not, and how to use it safely and on their own terms. No sales, no
jargon, and nothing for sale. Just digital literacy for everyday people.

So many folks feel either left behind by AI or uneasy about it, especially older
adults. This workshop meets them where they are. It demystifies the technology in
plain words, shows them they do not have to settle for whatever a company hands
them, and spends real time on staying safe: privacy, spotting scams and fake
voices, and keeping their own judgment in charge.

I can run it two ways, whichever fits your calendar:

- A single 50-minute talk, as a one-time evening or afternoon program, or
- A four-part series (about an hour each), going deeper: what AI is and is not,
  how to make it your own, how to stay safe and in control, and how to use it at
  home.

All I would need is a room, a projector or screen, and wifi. I bring everything
else, including a printed one-page take-home sheet for each attendee (a copy is
attached so you can see it). It is free, and it is genuinely for the community.

Could we find a date that works? I am happy to start with a single session as a
pilot so you can see how your patrons respond before committing to more.

Thank you for the work you do making knowledge open to everyone. That is exactly
the spirit this workshop is built in.

Warm regards,
Kenneth Bingham
[your email] · [your phone]
[optional: a one-line link, e.g., your LinkedIn or a short bio page]

## After you send
- If you do not hear back in about a week, send one short, friendly follow-up
  ("just floating this back to the top of your inbox"). One nudge, not more.
- When they say yes, ask for the expected audience size and whether most
  attendees will have a phone or laptop, so you can tailor the live demos.
- Offer to provide a short blurb and a title for their events calendar; libraries
  love when you make their promotion easy. Suggested blurb: *"It's Your AI: a
  free, plain-language workshop on what AI really is, how to make it your own, and
  how to stay safe and in control. No experience needed."*
